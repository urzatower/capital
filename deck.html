An asset with a supply schedule

Why sealed product, in three parts
Print run: finite

Supply ends on a schedule

A set is printed for a window and then never again. Sealed product is the only form of the asset whose float can shrink but never grow.

One-way attrition

The float shrinks every day

Every box opened leaves the sealed market permanently. Demand from players is a consumption of supply, not a transfer of it.

Widening buyer base

Demand arrives from outside

Crossover sets bring audiences who never played the game: film, comics and video game collectors bidding for the same boxes.

The asymmetry

A sealed box has a floor that is not zero, because the cards inside keep a resale value even in a bad outcome, and an upside that depends on how many boxes survive unopened. That shape is rare in collectibles and it is the reason this book exists.

The hazard, stated up front

Reprints. A publisher can reset the price of sealed product by printing the same cards again in a new frame or a new set. This is tracked per set, it is the main risk in the book, and it is why entries are small, dated and spread across release cycles.

The float only moves one way

How the return is actually produced

A print run closes

The publisher stops making a set. From that day the number of sealed boxes in existence can only fall.

Supply fixed
→

Boxes get opened

Players and collectors open product to get the cards. Every box opened leaves the sealed float permanently and cannot rejoin it.

Float shrinks
→

The audience widens

Films, games and crossover sets bring buyers who never played, bidding for the same shrinking float.

Demand broadens
→

Price carries the adjustment

With supply fixed and float falling, the only variable left that can move is price.

The return

What breaks the loop

A reprint. It is the one event that adds supply after the run has closed, and it is why the book spreads entries across sets and release cycles rather than concentrating in the most obvious one.

Why this is not a story about cards

The single cards inside the box are a separate market with its own supply, and they can be reprinted individually. The sealed box is the only wrapper whose contents are fixed at the moment the run ends.

What has to be true for this to work

  • Print runs keep ending, and the publisher keeps choosing scarcity over supply in at least part of the range.
  • Sealed product keeps being opened, so the float keeps falling faster than interest fades.
  • Crossover sets keep recruiting buyers from outside the game.
  • A public price feed keeps existing, so the asset can be marked rather than guessed.

What would prove it wrong, and how it would show up first

  • A set continues to fall after the product has fully arrived, which is a demand reading rather than a supply one.
  • Dealer bids widen away from dealer asks across several sets at once.
  • The gap between listed prices and completed sales grows.
  • New releases stop clearing at all. That is the signal the book watches monthly.

These are conditions, not forecasts. No projection of future value appears anywhere in this deck, because a book this size has no basis for one.

What the book buys

  • Crossover sets with an audience outside the game, where demand is not only from players.
  • Vintage and legacy sealed, out-of-print packs, tournament packs, starter and theme decks.
  • Small, dated entries rather than single large allocations, so the entry price is an average across a cycle rather than one print.

What it will not do

  • Open product. The moment a box is opened it stops being the asset that was bought.
  • Borrow. There is no leverage and no redemption pressure, so a drawdown never forces a sale.
  • Mark to an opinion. A position with no observed price is carried at cost.

Investment process

From sourcing to the daily mark
01

Source

Retail, marketplace and pre-order channels are watched for product below the level the price feed shows. Every order is captured with its number, seller and all-in cost.

02

Underwrite

A set is judged on audience outside the game, print window, and how much of the float is likely to be opened. Reprint exposure is written down before the purchase, not after.

03

Size

Entries are small and dated. No single acquisition is allowed to set the cost basis of a bucket, so the book averages across a release cycle.

04

Mark

Every position is repriced daily from the public market feed, with the same method used for the benchmark. Nothing is estimated.

05

Hold and monitor

Positions are held indefinitely. Reprint announcements, set rotations and float data are monitored, and a broken thesis is written up in a letter rather than quietly dropped.

The discipline that matters

Cost is recorded all-in, after discount, shipping and tax. A book that records the sticker price and ignores the tax flatters itself by a few percent on every entry, and that error compounds through the whole track record.

Capacity

The strategy is small by construction. Sealed product trades in a retail marketplace, so size is limited by what can be bought without moving the ask and sold without clearing the bid. Average capital at risk since inception is $2,541.

Valuation policy

  • A position is marked only from the transaction-derived market price for its product, latest observation per item, from the same public feed that builds the benchmark.
  • Dealer retail and dealer buylist are an ask and a bid, not a market price, and are never substituted.
  • A product the feed does not cover is carried at cost and flagged, never estimated.
  • Where a product has no sale history at all, such as a presale, the position may be marked to the lowest public ask and is labelled as an ask in the ledger.

Why this is stricter than it needs to be

An earlier version of this book valued positions by applying a growth rate to cost and flooring the result at cost, which made a loss arithmetically impossible. That method was deleted. Everything in this deck comes from observed prices or is shown at cost.

Risk management

What can go wrong, and what is done about it
Tracked per set

Reprint risk

The main hazard. A reprint resets the price of sealed product, so exposure is spread across release cycles and tracked per set.

Top position 35.7%

Concentration

The largest position is 35.7% of marked value and the largest bucket 38.6%. Concentration is disclosed rather than smoothed.

Mark is gross of exit cost

Liquidity

Sealed product trades retail. An exit carries marketplace fees, shipping and time, none of which are in the mark.

0 at cost

Marking risk

0 positions are carried at cost and one product is marked to an ask. Those are not transaction prices.

Sealed, graded on sight

Physical risk

Condition, storage, theft and damage. This is inventory, not a security, and it has to be kept as well as owned.

One counterparty to the asset

Single publisher

Most of the book depends on decisions taken by one publisher: print runs, reprints and set design.

No leverage and no outside capital means no forced selling, which is the only real defence a collectible book has during a drawdown. The maximum drawdown since inception is -21.2%.

The terminal behind the marks

The book is priced by infrastructure built for it: a price lake that collects vendor and marketplace observations every day, a set of indices computed from that lake, and a public terminal that exposes both. The benchmark in this deck is produced by the same code that draws the terminal's indices, so the fund cannot mark itself against a number nobody else can see.

The terminal is public at urzatower.github.io/capital/terminal. Anyone reading this deck can open it and check a mark.

Why it matters to an allocator

  • Reproducible marks. Every price in the book traces to a dated observation in the lake.
  • No survivorship in the benchmark. The index takes every box the feed prices, not a chosen basket.
  • Daily cadence. Marks, site, factsheet and letters refresh together, so no report is ever stale.
  • Auditability. Holdings, cost, marks and method are published in full rather than summarised.

Benchmark methodology

Sealed Magic booster box index

Sealed Magic booster box index

Capped value-weighted, chain-linked index of every Magic booster box priced by TCGplayer (market price; weekly archive from 2024-02-08, daily from 2026-08-28). No box above 10% of the basket; a price that moves more than fivefold between observations is treated as an error. Rebased to the book's inception.

Constituents249 booster boxes
First observation2024-02-08
Rebased100 at 2024-11-01
Since inception+48.6%
Book against benchmark+87.2%

Why this benchmark

A book of sealed product has no published index to be measured against, so the benchmark is built from the same price source that marks the book: every Magic booster box the feed covers, value-weighted with a cap so no single box drives the index, chain-linked across the boxes priced at both ends of each step.

Limits

  • Ask-marked and uncovered positions are in the book but not in the index.
  • The index is price only. It has no fees, shipping or tax, while the book's cost basis is all-in.
  • It covers Magic booster boxes. Product outside that, including the non-Magic holdings, has no index.
