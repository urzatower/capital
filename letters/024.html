Letter 024 · 12 September 2026 · Miami, Florida

The box that has not shipped

On 2 September a buyer posted that his Hobbit collector booster box, ordered from Amazon with a ship date of 7 September that had already been pushed to 12 September, had just been cancelled "due to availability". A friend's identical order was still alive. Five days later a second thread: three of four Hobbit scene bundles cancelled on the morning they were due to arrive, preorders placed months earlier, and a support supervisor telling the buyer that the fulfilment centre cancelled because the publisher had not met its latest shipping contract. That last sentence is hearsay twice over, a poster quoting a phone call. I am repeating it because the fund owns eleven lines of this product and the question of whether the publisher is shipping what it sold is not academic to me.

The Hobbit released on 14 August. Four weeks later the market is still deciding what it is worth, and the deciding is being done by people who in some cases do not yet hold the box.

what the tape says about the delay

The preorder curve is the clearest record I have. The weekly TCGplayer archive starts pricing the Hobbit play booster box on 4 May at 245 dollars. It slid to 221 by late May, 203 by mid June, 184 in early July and 180 at the end of July, then turned up to 193 on 17 August, three days after release, and closed August at 183. The daily lake, which starts 28 August, has it at 161.33 today, off a high of 173.57 nine days ago. Card Kingdom asks 224.99 for the same box and bids 146. The collector box ran the other way: 900 on 4 May, 729 in mid June, back to 823 in early July and 832 in mid August, 816 at month end, 787.38 today against a dealer ask of 849.99 and a listed median on TCGplayer of 899.99.

Read those two curves together and the delay story is visible in the numbers. A play box that everybody can buy fell a third from its preorder price and kept falling after release, because the product arrived. A collector box that some buyers are still waiting for held within ten percent of where it was priced in May, with the ask sitting a hundred dollars over the last trade. The listed median is what sellers want. The market price is what buyers paid. The gap between them, 14 percent on the collector box today, is roughly the premium for actually having one.

Underneath the boxes, the set's singles index is down 21.8 percent in a month. The most expensive card in the set is a serialized Smaug the Magnificent that the feed prices at 24,500 dollars, and I have said before what I think of value weights that one card can move, which is why the index caps any single constituent at a tenth of the basket. Even capped, the direction is not ambiguous. A modelling thread on 2 September put the mean gross singles value of a Hobbit collector pack at 62.74 dollars against a 38.39 median, and worked out that at the tracked street price of about 77 dollars a pack, a buyer breaks even 22 percent of the time. That is the arithmetic of a lottery whose tickets are still in transit.

Two smaller signals from the same week. A thread on 3 September warned about a preorder scam on eBay in which sellers list product more than thirty days out, never ship, and hide behind the platform's claim window. And on 28 August, before either cancellation thread, somebody posted that Reality Fracture play booster supply may be tight at release. I could not read the source it cited. I note it because the same shape is forming around the next set before this one has finished arriving.

what the fund holds and what it is marked at

The Hobbit sleeve is 3,228.04 of cost, the largest sleeve in the book after vintage, and it is marked tonight at 2,968.53, which is 8.04 percent below cost. Five play booster boxes bought between 13 May and 24 August at 197.94 to 207.05 each are all marked at 161.33, so between 18 and 22 percent under water. The collector booster display bought on 18 July at 839.85 and the collector booster box bought on 13 August at 795.00 are both marked at 787.38, down 6.25 and 0.96 percent. The two scene boxes bought on 1 May at 44.93 are marked at 59.19 and 59.67, up 32 percent, which is the only green in the sleeve and is the product Amazon is now cancelling. Twenty eight sleeved set booster packs are held at cost because nothing prices them.

I want to be precise about what those marks are. Every one of them is the last TCGplayer market trade for the product, taken once a day. They are not what I could sell for. On the play box the dealer bid is 146, nine percent below the mark. On the collector box there is no dealer bid at all. The book is marked to a price that reflects the delivery of a product that, for some buyers, has not been delivered.

the next set, and what the publisher has said about it

Reality Fracture releases on 2 October, prerelease from 25 September, with the story already finished on 10 September. The publisher's own product article is the most useful document I have read this month and I will summarise it rather than quote it. Play boosters at 5.49, thirty to a box. Collector boosters at 26.99, twelve to a box. A Secret Lair bundle at 89.99 with six play boosters, two collector boosters and two foil promos from a set of ten. The set is 71 commons, 109 uncommons, 64 rares and 26 mythics, plus 132 "echoed pair" cards, mirrored versions of the same character from two realities. The chase treatments have published counts, which is rare enough to write down: roughly 6,000 copies of each rare facet foil and 3,000 of each mythic in the shattered mirror series, and 500 serialized copies of the headliner, appearing in under one percent of collector boosters.

The mechanic the whole set is built around is called Empower Jace. It creates blue Jace planeswalker tokens that are not legendary, each with a minus one to surveil and a minus three to draw a card, and the story frames it as Jace bringing versions of himself to your battles. The story itself has a fractured Jace, called the Theorist, as the antagonist, remaking the multiverse from an alternate reality after his apparent death, with Liliana split into a Faultless echo who follows him and a Repentant original who does not. Jace is the face of the Multiverse Reforged commander deck. A thread on 6 September predicted that card and its protect-all-planeswalkers ability before the publisher confirmed it. Another on 1 September asked whether this is where Jace dies. I do not know, and I have learned from twenty three of these letters that the story matters to the price of exactly one card at a time, briefly.

The preorder curve here is already interesting. The play booster box was 248 on 4 May, 199 in late May, 147 in early July, 130 on 17 August, 148 at month end, and the daily lake has it at 190.95 today, up 29 percent in a week, while Card Kingdom asks 144.99. The collector box went 700, 569, 488, 478, 476, then 533 at the end of August and 530.10 today, against a dealer ask of 524.99. The Secret Lair bundle went the opposite direction to everything else, 120 in May to 198 in July and 192 now, more than double its list price before a single one has shipped, on a thread's reasoning that a bundle with Secret Lair in the name and a set about Liliana and Jace will contain an exclusive version of one of them. The publisher's article says two promos from ten. It does not say which.

A set index of the twenty preview cards the feed can already price is down 23 percent in a month. That is the usual decay of spoiler-season asks into release-week reality, and it is the reason the fund's first Reality Fracture position, a collector box bought on 7 September at 641.95, is marked tonight at 530.10, down 17.42 percent five days after purchase. The play box bought the next day at 149.79 is marked at 190.95, up 27.48 percent. Net, the sleeve is 791.74 of cost against 721.05 of value. I paid the spoiler price for one and the trough price for the other, a day apart, and the marks are telling me exactly that.

Jace, across the years I can actually see

Since the set is about him, I pulled every printing of Jace, the Mind Sculptor the archive can price. The honest caveat first: the weekly TCGplayer archive begins on 8 February 2024, so anything I say about earlier years is not from data I hold. The corpus does carry a thread from June 2016 remembering the day five years before when the card was banned in Standard, which dates the ban to June 2011 and is the only pre-2024 fact about Jace I will put in this letter.

The card has eight paper printings. The original from Worldwake was 26.64 dollars in February 2024, 21.27 at the start of 2025, 21.95 at the start of 2026, and 24.88 on 31 August. Its low over the whole archive is 20.58, its high is the first observation. The foil trades at 221.95. The three masters-set reprints move together and lower: Eternal Masters 19.39 to 14.77 to 17.34 to 18.43, Masters 25 18.86 to 14.08 to 17.78 to 17.31, Double Masters 19.33 to 15.17 to 17.91 to 17.83. Every one of them bottomed at the start of 2025 and recovered about a fifth since. The Secret Lair version, the only printing whose supply was fixed the day it sold, went 321.76 in February 2024, 234.28 at the start of 2025, 381.41 at the start of this year and 294.39 at the end of August, with the foil at 2,899.99. Nothing else in the set of eight has a range like that, and nothing else had a closed print run.

The most instructive printing is the newest. The Bloomburrow Commander version entered the archive on 15 July 2024 at 54.11 dollars, was 15.18 by January 2025, and is 23.89 now. That is a launch premium of roughly three and a half times the settled price, gone in six months, on a card that had already been printed seven times. If you want a prior for what the Reality Fracture Jace cards do, that is the one I would use rather than anything the story implies. The forum is already ahead of it: a thread on 8 September flagged Jace, Cunning Castaway at one dollar, with forty copies sold in a day against an average of one, because its ultimate works with the new tokens. That is what a sub-two-dollar card looks like the week before it is either a real spec or a footnote.

the book

The book stands tonight at 9,032.98 against 9,229.92 of invested capital, unrealized minus 2.13 percent, with 36 of 44 positions carrying an observed price and 94.2 percent of cost marked. Vintage is up 1.52 percent, the Tolkien sleeve up 35.0, Spider-Man up 14.51. The Hobbit is down 8.04, Marvel down 9.39, Reality Fracture down 8.93. The money-weighted return since inception is minus 2.13 percent, an annualised minus 9.19 on an average capital at risk of 921 dollars. The NAV per unit figure the site also reports, plus 131.07 percent, is chained over positions marked at both ends and says more about the vintage boxes bought two years ago than about anything I did this month.

What I did this month was buy the two most recent sets at the two points on the preorder curve I have just described, and I own a collector box of one of them at a price the market has not seen since June, delivered, in my hands, which is more than some of the people in those threads can say. The lesson of the letter is not that preorders are bad. It is that the preorder curve is a real curve, that it has a shape I can now see across two sets, and that the publisher's ability to deliver on the date is one more input to it that I had been treating as a constant.

The box that has not shipped is still priced as though it had.
