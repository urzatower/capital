Investment objective

Long-term growth of capital through ownership of sealed trading card product held for scarcity. The book seeks to compound the value of unopened product across print cycles rather than to trade it, and measures itself against the Sealed Magic booster box index.

Principal investment strategies

The book invests, under normal circumstances, substantially all of its capital in sealed trading card product: booster boxes, collector displays, individual packs, decks, bundles and scene boxes. The universe is Magic: The Gathering and its Universes Beyond crossovers, with a small allocation to other sealed collectible product. It is long only, unlevered and self-funded.

Product is selected on three criteria. First, an audience beyond the game, so that demand does not depend only on people who play. Second, a supply schedule that has ended or will end, since a print run that has closed cannot be extended. Third, an entry price below the level the public price feed shows, with purchases made in small dated amounts across a release cycle rather than in single allocations.

Nothing is opened. Opening a box converts the asset into its contents and removes the scarcity that was bought. Positions are held indefinitely and there is no target price at which a sale is triggered.

Valuation. Each position is repriced every business day from the transaction-derived market price for that product, taken from the same public feed used to build the benchmark. Dealer asking prices and dealer buylist bids are not substituted for a market price. A product the feed does not cover is carried at cost and disclosed, and a product with no sale history at all may be marked to the lowest public asking price and is labelled as such in the ledger.

Principal risks

There is no assurance the book will meet its objective. The value of the assets can fall, and a holder can lose part or all of the money committed. The risks below are listed alphabetically, not in order of importance, so that they can be compared with the risks disclosed by other vehicles.

Concentration risk. The book is not diversified. The largest position is 41.1% of marked value and the largest strategy bucket, LOTR: Tales of Middle-earth, is 44.9%. A single set, and in practice a single box, drives the reported return. Concentration of this kind means one product's price can decide the whole result.

Counterparty and provenance risk. Sealed product is bought from marketplaces, retailers and private sellers. A box is a promise that its contents are original and untouched, and that promise has never been verified on any unit in the book. Counterfeit shrink wrap, resealed product and non-delivery are live hazards, and the recourse on a private sale is limited.

Liquidity risk. Sealed product trades in a retail marketplace, one unit at a time, to individual buyers. There is no market maker and no bid that must be honoured. Selling a large position at the marked price is not assured, and the marks in this document are gross of marketplace fees, shipping, insurance and any grading or authentication cost.

Marking risk. 9 of 51 positions have no observed market price and are carried at cost. One product with no sale history is marked to the lowest public asking price. Carrying a position at cost is not a statement that it is worth cost.

Physical risk. The assets are objects. Condition, humidity, sunlight, storage, theft, fire and handling all bear on value, and a sealed box that is damaged cannot be restored. There is no custodian and no insurance is assumed in the marks.

Publisher risk. Most of the book depends on decisions taken by one publisher: how much is printed, when a set is reprinted, how product is allocated, and whether announced product ships on time. None of those decisions is disclosed in advance to holders of the product.

Reprint risk. The principal risk. A publisher may reprint a set, or the cards inside it, in a new product or a new frame. A reprint can reset the price of sealed product permanently and without notice, and no holder is compensated for it.

Single-market risk. Demand comes from collectors and players spending discretionary income. A prolonged fall in that spending, or a shift of attention to another hobby, would reduce demand across every position at once. The book has no exposure that would offset that.

Valuation-lag risk. Prices come from a public feed that observes marketplace transactions. Thinly traded product can go weeks between observations, so a mark may reflect a stale transaction rather than what a buyer would pay today.

Purchase and sale

Interests are not offered, sold or redeemed. There is no subscription process, no minimum, no share class and no secondary market. Assets are bought from public retailers and marketplaces with the manager's own money and are held in his possession.

Tax information

Because nothing has been sold, no gain has been realised and no tax has arisen on the book's returns. A future sale of collectible property is generally a taxable event, and in the United States gains on collectibles are taxed at a rate that can exceed the long-term rate for securities. Sales tax paid on purchase is included in cost basis. This is a description, not tax advice.

Payments to intermediaries

None. Nobody is paid to distribute, recommend or place this book, because there is nothing to place.
